# Pick the form

Pick by the relationship the takeaway is about, not by what looks impressive. Use the simplest form that shows it honestly.

| The takeaway is about... | Use | Not |
|---|---|---|
| One number that matters | A number tile: value, label, comparison (vs last period, vs target) | A gauge or a donut with one slice |
| Change over time | Line chart. Bars if there are under about 12 periods and each is its own event | Area stacks for more than 3 series |
| Ranking or comparing items | Horizontal bar chart, sorted, labels on the left | Vertical bars with rotated labels, pie |
| Part of a whole (2 to 5 parts) | One stacked bar, or a sorted bar chart | Pie past 5 slices, 3D anything |
| Part of a whole over time | 100% stacked bars or small multiples | Stacked area with many layers |
| Many items, same measure, over time | Small multiples with one shared scale | One spaghetti chart with 8 lines |
| Spread or distribution | Histogram, strip or dot plot, box plot for experts | Average alone |
| Relationship between two measures | Scatter plot, labeled outliers | Dual-axis line chart |
| Gap between two values (before/after, plan/actual) | Dot plot with a connecting line (dumbbell) | Two grouped bars per item when there are many items |
| Progress to a goal | Bar with a target line, or a bullet chart | Gauge |
| Flow between stages | Funnel as sorted bars with drop-off labeled, or a Sankey for real branching | Sankey for a straight funnel |
| Exact values people will look up | A table with aligned numbers. Add inline bars or sparklines if it helps | A chart |
| Where (location matters) | Map, only if location is the point | Map when a sorted list would answer it |

## Before you chart

- **Is a chart needed?** One or two numbers are a sentence or a tile. A lookup is a table.
- **How many series?** Past 5 or 6, highlight the one or two that matter and put the rest in gray, or split into small multiples.
- **What will the reader compare?** Put those things next to each other on a shared scale.

## Dashboards

- Top row: the few numbers that decide what to do next, each with a comparison.
- Then the charts that explain those numbers, biggest question first.
- Every chart has a takeaway title. A dashboard of untitled charts says nothing.
- Same scales and same colors for the same thing everywhere on the page.
