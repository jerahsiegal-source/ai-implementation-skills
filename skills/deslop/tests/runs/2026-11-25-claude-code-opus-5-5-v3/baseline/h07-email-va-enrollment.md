Dear addressee:

We're writing about a change in your enrollment for the term that started September 1, 1996. Because of this change, we may have paid you more than you were entitled to.

What our records show

On November 5, 1996, you dropped from 12 credit hours to 6.

What we did

Starting December 1, 1996, we reduced your payments to $202.00 a month. A separate letter about this change will follow, and it will tell you whether you owe a debt.

What we need from you

Please send us a statement explaining why you cut back your credit hours. In most cases we need evidence to back up your statement. If you don't have any, tell us why. (See the enclosure "Changes in Your Enrollment.")

When we need it

Please send your statement and any evidence within 30 days.

What happens next

If we accept your statement, we won't reduce your payments back to the start of the term. If a debt has already been created, you'll still need to repay it, but it won't get any larger.

If we don't accept your statement and evidence, we'll reduce your payments effective September 1, 1996, the first day of the term. That will create a debt you'll have to repay.

If we don't hear from you within 30 days, we'll also reduce your payments effective September 1, 1996, the first day of the term, and that will create a debt you'll have to repay. Even after we do this, we'll still consider any information you send within one year of the date of this letter. If you can show a good reason why you couldn't meet that deadline, we may give you more time.

Your rights

You have basic rights you can use before we make a final decision on your claim. (See the enclosure "Basic Rights.")
