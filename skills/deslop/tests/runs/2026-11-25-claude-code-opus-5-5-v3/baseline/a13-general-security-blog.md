A lot of small business owners figure cybersecurity is a big-company problem. Attackers don't think that way. They run automated tools that sweep the internet for weak passwords, unpatched software and exposed systems, and those tools treat a Fortune 500 firm the same as a five-person bakery. One successful breach can cost a small business its customer data or the money in its bank accounts. It can also lock up the point-of-sale system and stop sales for days.

Most attacks get in through basic gaps, and you can close those without a big budget. Four steps block most common threats:

- Turn on multi-factor authentication.
- Use a password manager.
- Keep your software updated.
- Back up your files somewhere that isn't always connected to the network.

Phishing emails are still the most common way attackers get in, so teach your staff to spot them. That training costs about an afternoon.

Good security also protects your relationships. Customers, partners and payment processors more and more expect you to have reasonable security in place, and one incident can wipe out trust you spent years building. If you handle security as part of normal operations and don't wait for an emergency, you can keep serving your customers without worrying about the next breach.
