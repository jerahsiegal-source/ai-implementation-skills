Last year we launched a product I was sure would work. We spent eight months building it and had a polished demo, a launch week plan and a press list. Three months later we shut it down.

The product wasn't bad. Our mistake was building the whole thing before we'd asked anyone to pay for it.

We did talk to customers early on, but we asked the wrong questions. "Would you use this?" gets you a polite yes every time. Nobody told us "I'd pay for this today." We counted the politeness as demand.

Launch week showed the gap. Hundreds of people signed up, and almost none of them came back after day two.

If I did it again, I'd ask for money before building. A pre-order, a deposit or a signed letter of intent tells you more than a hundred interviews.

Judge the signal by what people do, not what they say. Sign-ups are a vanity number; week-four retention is the one that counts.

I'd also launch smaller and sooner. We spent eight months polishing something we could have tested in eight weeks with a rough prototype.

Finally, name the assumption that would kill the product and test that one first. Ours was "people will change their workflow for this." We tested it last.
