Last year we launched a product I was sure would work.

We spent eight months building it. We had a polished demo, a launch week plan and a press list. Three months later we shut it down.

The product wasn't bad. The problem was that we built the whole thing before asking anyone to pay for it.

We did talk to customers early on, but we asked the wrong questions. Ask "Would you use this?" and you get a polite yes every time. Nobody told us "I'd pay for this today." We counted the politeness as demand.

Launch week showed the gap. Hundreds of people signed up. Almost none came back after day two.

If I did it again, I'd change four things.

1. Ask for money before you build. A pre-order, a deposit or a signed letter of intent tells you more than a hundred interviews.
2. Watch what people do, not what they say. Sign-ups are vanity. Week-four retention is the real number.
3. Launch smaller and sooner. We spent eight months polishing something we could have tested in eight weeks with a rough prototype.
4. Find the assumption that would kill the product and test it first. Ours was "people will change their workflow for this." We tested it last.

It was an expensive failure. I still think I learned more from it than from any launch that went well.

If you're about to launch something, what's the one assumption you haven't tested yet?
