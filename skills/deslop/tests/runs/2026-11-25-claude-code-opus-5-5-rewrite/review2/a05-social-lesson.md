Last year we launched a product I was sure would work. We spent eight months building it and had a polished demo, a launch week plan and a press list. Three months after launch we shut it down.

The product wasn't bad. We had built the whole thing before we'd asked anyone to pay for it.

We did talk to customers early on, but we asked the wrong questions. "Would you use this?" gets you a polite yes every time. Nobody told us "I'd pay for this today." We counted the politeness as demand.

Launch week showed the gap. Hundreds of people signed up, and almost none of them came back after day two.

Next time I'd do four things differently.

1. Ask for money before you build. A pre-order, a deposit, or a signed letter of intent tells you more than a hundred interviews.
2. Judge the signal by what people do, not what they say. Sign-ups are vanity. Week-four retention is the real number.
3. Launch smaller and sooner. We spent eight months polishing something we could have tested in eight weeks with a rough prototype.
4. Name the assumption that would kill the product, then test that one first. Ours was "people will change their workflow for this." We tested it last.
