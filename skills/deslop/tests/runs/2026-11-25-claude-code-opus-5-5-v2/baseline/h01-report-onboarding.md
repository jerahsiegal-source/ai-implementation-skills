Three months ago, my colleague Andrew Maier and I were asked to rethink how 18F onboards new hires.

We needed to. 18F has grown fast. In the past year and a half we've gone from 15 people to more than 160, [spread out](https://18f.gsa.gov/2015/10/15/best-practices-for-distributed-teams/) across the United States.

A new group of hires joins every two weeks. They may live in different places, and they bring different skills, backgrounds, and ways of learning. Whatever their job, each of them has to learn how things work at 18F, in the federal government, and on their own team and projects.

Over the past three months we've released several products to help new hires settle in. This post covers what we built and why it works well.

## Onboarding as building blocks

From the start, we thought of onboarding as a set of blocks that connect to each other. This is how we sketched those blocks back in September:

Some things every 18F employee needs to know. For example: what the [Hatch Act is](https://www.oge.gov/web/oge.nsf/Outside%20Employment%20and%20Activities/88E8E6E11682F97885257E96006364FF?opendocument), where to file for health insurance, and how to add a bio to our website.

New hires also need information about where they work. Someone who works remotely needs to know how to dial into our weekly all-hands meeting. Someone in Chicago needs to know where the bathrooms and printers are in the Chicago office.

Every 18F employee is also on a team. A new Design team member has to learn the software the Design team uses. A new Talent team member has to learn how the Talent team handles recruiting.

Last, each person is staffed on a project, and they need to get up to speed on it before they can start work on something like [betaFEC](https://beta.fec.gov/) or [Every Kid in a Park](https://everykidinapark.gov/).

So we had four basic categories: 18F, location, team, and project. What we didn't know was what information belonged in each one. We spent two weeks looking at how other organizations bring new employees on board. Then we interviewed 17 people across 18F. We asked directors and team leads questions like these:

-   What do you want every new hire on your team to know?
-   When do you first talk to a new hire on your team?
-   What tools do you use to share information with your team?
-   What should 18F teach every new hire, whatever their team?
-   Who outside 18F does onboarding well?
