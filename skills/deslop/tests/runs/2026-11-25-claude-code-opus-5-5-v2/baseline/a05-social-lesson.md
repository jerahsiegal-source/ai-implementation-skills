Last year we launched a product I was certain would work. Three months later we shut it down.

It took eight months to build. We had a polished demo, a plan for launch week, and a press list. The product itself wasn't bad. The mistake was that we built all of it before anyone had been asked to pay for it.

We did talk to customers early. We just asked the wrong question. Ask "would you use this?" and people will politely say yes every time. Not one of them said "I'd pay for this today," and we treated the politeness as demand.

Launch week made that obvious. Hundreds of people signed up, and almost none of them came back after day two.

If I did it again:

Ask for money before you build anything. A pre-order, a deposit or a signed letter of intent will tell you more than a hundred interviews.

Watch what people do and ignore what they say. Sign-ups are a vanity number. The one that matters is week-four retention.

Launch smaller and earlier. We spent eight months polishing something a rough prototype could have tested in eight weeks.

Work out which assumption would kill the product if it's wrong, and test that one first. Ours was "people will change their workflow for this," and it was the last thing we tested.

It was an expensive failure. I still think it taught me more than any of my launches that went well.

So if you're about to launch something, which assumption haven't you tested yet?
